For ten weeks in the summer of 2021, I completed an internship with USFWS through their Directorate Fellowship Program. During this experience, I networked with other conservation professionals, learned about the USFWS mission, and completed an independent project. 

In this project, I created a visual risk analysis surface map of the southeast United States, in which risk to listed species from chemical hazards is shown on a numeric color scale. 
In addition to creating the map deliverables for the project, I also documented all data, saved them, and shared project outcomes with relevant audiences of restoration practitioners and oil and hazardous spill response professionals.

## Project Description 

Natural Resource restoration needs often arise from oil, gas, or chemical contamination. Industrial accidents or releases of chemicals can affect land, air, and water resources and have a host of effects on the human environment system, including the loss of recreational opportunities, ecosystem services, and biodiversity. 

This interactive map and risk assessment framework were created to give spatial context to past restoration projects and preemptively locate sites of potential future accidents. The risk assessment compiles relevant locations of oil, gas, and chemical based hazards as well as threatened and endangered species distributions. These are then organized spatially within a numerical ranking system and displayed within an interactive map that can be incorporated into restoration planning and implementation.
