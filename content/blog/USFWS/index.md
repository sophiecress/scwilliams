In my role as a spatial ecologist with the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, I create static and interactive maps, maintain spatial databases, and conduct spatial analyses to manage data in the public Trust and use it to tell stories about Deepwater Horizon settlement-funded restoration projects. A selection of them are described below.

However, many of these products are intended for internal audiences only.  I am always happy to discuss this work in lieu of sharing it publicly. Please contact me to learn more!

## Maps and Spatial Analysis

1. Static maps for diverse documents and presentations
I regularly make static maps as figures for internal documents and presentations. These maps are carefully designed for various audiences, from restoration practitioners to other USFWS programs and the American public. 

2. Basemaps for the 2025 Programmatic Review of Deepwater Horizon
The Deepwater Horizon Natural Resource Damage Assessment Programmatic Review provides an in-depth look at restoration progress in the Gulf through the end of 2025. Maps to show project locations and distributions are part of the graphic layout of this document, which summarizes restoration progress by each type of natural resource. This document will be publicly released in 2026.

3. Spatial tools in R and Python
I employ scripts and tools in R and Python as needed to gather relevant information about restoration to share with USFWS decision makers. For example, I applied the [wqReport R package](https://www.usgs.gov/software/wqreport) to summarize information about water quality on USFWS National Wildlife Refuges in Florida.

4. National Geospatial Training Workshop
In 2023, I attended the USFWS National Geospatial Training Workshop at the National Conservation Training Center. At this training, I worked through applied exercises using various geospatial tools, including Esri's ArcGIS Online Web Applications, StoryMaps, and ExperienceBuilder, as well as QGIS and Python applications in GIS.
